# Clock Rings — Privacy Policy

**Last updated: June 11, 2026**

Clock Rings is an independent app built for shift and postal workers. This policy
explains what the app does and does not do with your information. The short version:
**Clock Rings has no account, no ads, and no analytics. The developer operates no
servers and never receives your data.**

---

## What we collect

**Nothing is collected by the developer.** Clock Rings has no user accounts, no sign-in,
no advertising, no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs, and no developer-operated
backend. The developer cannot see your punches, hours, pay figures, leave, or location.

## Where your data lives

Everything you enter — punches, schedules, pay rates, leave, holidays, alarms, and
settings — is stored:

- **On your device**, and
- **In your own private iCloud** (Apple CloudKit private database and iCloud
  Key‑Value Storage), so it can sync across your iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch.

iCloud sync uses **your** Apple Account and Apple's infrastructure. It is governed by
[Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). The developer has no
access to your iCloud data.

## Location (used only for weather)

If you turn on the optional Weather feature, Clock Rings asks for **"When In Use"**
location access to show local conditions on the clock view. Your coordinates are
**rounded to roughly one kilometer** and used solely to request current weather. The
rounded location is sent to the weather provider to fetch a forecast and is **not
stored, not linked to your identity, and never used for advertising or tracking.**

Weather providers:

- **Open‑Meteo** (`open-meteo.com`) — the default provider. The request contains only
  the rounded latitude/longitude. See Open‑Meteo's terms/privacy at
  [open-meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/).
- **Apple Weather (WeatherKit)** — used only if you switch to it; governed by Apple's
  privacy policy.

If you never enable Weather, the app does not request your location and no location
data ever leaves your device.

## Network connections the app makes

Clock Rings contacts only:

1. **Apple iCloud** — to sync your own data across your devices (your Apple Account).
2. **Open‑Meteo** *(or Apple WeatherKit)* — to fetch weather, only if you enable it
   (rounded location only, as above).
3. **Apple (`apple.com`)** — a lightweight check used to verify accurate time for the
   clock. No personal data is sent.

There are no other network connections, and no data is sent to the developer or any
advertiser.

## In‑app purchases (optional tips)

Clock Rings is free. You may leave an optional tip to support development. Tips are
processed entirely by **Apple's App Store**; the developer receives only Apple's
standard sales reports and **never** sees your payment details.

## Pay stub compare (optional)

The optional **Earnings Statement Compare** feature lets you paste your earnings-statement
text from LiteBlue ePayroll and lines its numbers up against the app's. The text is
parsed **entirely on your device** — it is **never uploaded, never stored, and never
seen by the developer.** The app automatically masks your name, employee ID, and any
SSN or address in the text it shows back to you. Clock Rings does not use the camera.

## USPS Virtual Timecard

Clock Rings is **independent and not affiliated with, endorsed by, or associated with
the United States Postal Service (USPS).** The "USPS Virtual Timecard" button simply
opens the public USPS web timecard (`vtc.usps.gov`) in Safari — the app does **not** log
in, store credentials, or read anything from that page. The "Reconcile with Virtual
Timecard" feature compares only the hours **you** paste in, on your device; nothing is
uploaded.

## Children

Clock Rings is not directed to children and collects no personal information from
anyone.
